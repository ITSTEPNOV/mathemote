About

Mathemote is a free tool that uses Stepnov’s Method to show the scale of a number through facial expressions. The exact number stays visible.

Why scale is hard to see

Computers and AI can return numbers that are far larger or smaller than the ones we use every day. Scientific notation makes these numbers shorter to write. A face adds a visual cue to the scale of the result.

How the method works

Each window covers 15 decimal orders. One step on the scale multiplies or divides the value by ten. The mouth changes every three steps, and the brows pick one of the three positions within that group.

  1. Smile
  2. Neutral Mouth
  3. Downturned Mouth
  4. Pressed Lips
  5. Bared Teeth

Brows within a group

  1. Furrowed Brows
  2. Neutral Brows
  3. Raised Brows

As you type a number in Auto window mode, the scale moves to its order and keeps the number as typed.

Three examples

Education

Compare a million, a billion and a trillion. Each is a thousand times the previous one. In the initial window, the mouth moves to the next group and the brows stay the same.

  • 106
  • 109
  • 1012

Artificial intelligence

Suppose an AI tool counts 100 million possible configurations, then counts one trillion after you change a setting. A different face can flag that jump while the exact counts remain visible.

Space navigation

Compare a trip to the Moon with a trip to Proxima Centauri. The face adds a cue to interstellar distance and travel time. The flight demo still calculates time from distance and speed.

Use case

Who might use it?

Mathemote is designed for people without specialist maths training. It can be explored in education, AI, scientific computing, programming, engineering, data visualisation and interfaces that offer several ways to read the same result. How easily people learn and read the expressions is still to be tested.

Ways to read the result

The Sequential standard shows the mouth first and the brows second. The Simultaneous standard shows both together.

The demo also supplies selectable text and can read it aloud with a compatible local voice. These channels describe the same number and expression.

Authorship
